## Visual Design Direction

**Constraint:** light theme only — no dark mode.

**Subject:** a recruiting/staffing company bridging two audiences — students starting out and professionals moving on — plus the employers who hire both. The design should feel warmer and more human than the generic navy-and-white corporate-recruiting template every competitor defaults to, without losing professional credibility.

**Colour palette:**

| Name | Where it's used |
|---|---|
| Ink | Body text and headings — a warm near-black rather than pure black |
| Paper | Page background — a warm off-white rather than stark white |
| Brand | Primary colour — deep teal-green, signalling growth and trust. Buttons and links. |
| Accent | Warm orange, used sparingly — CTA highlights, the signature path line, active states |
| Surface | Cards and alternating section backgrounds |
| Border | Dividers and input outlines |

**Typography:**
- **Display** (headings) — a warm serif or slab face, used with restraint at large sizes only. Gives the page personality that a sans-only recruiting site lacks.
- **Body** — a clean humanist sans for all paragraph and interface text, chosen for legibility at small sizes.
- **Numeric** — a mono or semi-mono face reserved for stat counters and process-step numbers. Reinforces that placement results are data, not marketing copy.

**Layout concept:** generous whitespace on the warm paper background, alternating zig-zag split sections, and cards that sit on a soft surface tone rather than being outlined in heavy borders and shadows.

**Signature element — "The Path":** a single continuous curved line that appears in the Home hero background, reappears threading through the "How It Works" step icons, and again connecting the stat counters — a literal visual path from "candidate" to "placement" that ties the whole site together, rather than a decorative flourish repeated for its own sake. It draws itself in as each section scrolls into view, and appears fully drawn without animating for visitors who have reduced-motion turned on.

**Numbered step markers** (Process / How-It-Works sections) are justified here — these are real ordered sequences (Apply → Match → Interview → Placement), not decoration, so numbering them is appropriate. We don't number things that aren't sequences.

## 1. Home

**Goal:** convert a first-time visitor (student or professional) into a lead within 10 seconds of scroll, while also signalling credibility to employers.

1. **Hero** — Warm paper background rather than the dark-gradient-over-stock-photo default, which keeps the human tone. **"The Path" opens here**, curving behind the headline. Display-face headline, left-aligned, subheadline below, and two side-by-side calls to action: "Find a Job" (solid) and "Hire Talent" (outline). No rotating or carousel text — one static headline converts better and loads faster. On mobile the image stacks below the text; side by side on desktop.
2. **Dual-Path Split** — Two cards side by side: "For Job Seekers" and "For Employers", each with an icon, a two-line pitch, and its own call to action. Stacked full-width on mobile.
3. **Services Grid** — Five cards, each with an icon, title, one-line description, and a "Learn more" link. One column on mobile, two on tablet, three on desktop.
4. **Why Choose Us** — Four to five icon-and-text tiles (Personalised Matching, Verified Employers, Fast Turnaround, End-to-End Support), icon above text.
5. **Success Metrics** — A full-width coloured band with four counters set in the numeric face; **"The Path" reappears here**, threading between them. Counters: Candidates Placed, Partner Companies, Years of Experience, Placement Success. Numbers count up as the band scrolls into view. Two-by-two grid on mobile, a single row on desktop.
6. **Testimonials** — Cards that swipe horizontally on mobile and sit in a row on desktop. Real photos beat stock; where we have none, fall back to an initials avatar.
7. **How It Works** — Four numbered steps (Apply → Match → Interview → Placement). **"The Path" connects each step icon** horizontally on desktop, vertically on mobile.
8. **Final CTA Band** — Solid brand background, one centred button, one action only, no competing links.
9. **Footer** — see [Shared Components](#shared-components-all-pages).

## 2. About Us

**Goal:** build trust — who we are, why we started, why we're credible.

1. **Page Header** — Smaller than Home's, on the surface tone. Display-face page title "About Us", a one-line tagline, and a breadcrumb.
2. **Our Story** — Zig-zag split with the image on one side and the company origin and mission on the other, with one key phrase set large as a pull-quote. Image stacks above the text on mobile.
3. **Mission & Values** — Three to four tiles (Integrity, Candidate-First, Speed, Long-Term Partnerships), each an icon plus a short line.
4. **Stats Strip** — The exact same stat counters as Home, drawn from the same source so the numbers never disagree between pages.
5. **Meet the Team** *(only if real team photos exist)* — A grid of member cards: photo, name, role, one-line bio. **Skip the section entirely if we don't have real photos** — stock headshots undermine the trust this page exists to build.
6. **Why Students & Professionals Trust Us** — The same two-card pattern as the Home dual-path block, rewritten as trust statements rather than calls to action.
7. **Company Facts** — A small factual line at the foot of the page: registered name, founding year, office. Cheap credibility that almost no competitor bothers with.
8. **CTA Band** — Same component as Home with different wording.

## 3. Services (Overview)

**Goal:** let a visitor compare all five offerings and pick the one relevant to them, or get in touch about a bundled need.

1. **Page Header** — Same pattern as About: title "Our Services" plus an intro sentence framing the five offerings as one connected pipeline (source → vet → place → verify → upskill). **"The Path" runs horizontally behind that phrase** as a preview of the sequence to come.
2. **Which Service Fits You?** — A simple grid showing which of the three audiences (Students, Professionals, Employers) each service is for. **This sits above the detail rows** — it's the fastest way for a visitor to find their own door. Collapses to stacked label-and-value pairs on mobile rather than shrinking a table.
3. **Services Detail Rows** — Each of the five in its own zig-zag row, alternating image-left and image-right, with a two-to-three-line description, a "not for you if…" line pointing to the right alternative, and an "Explore" link. Every row stacks image-above-text on mobile regardless of its desktop order.
4. **CTA Band** — "Not sure which service fits? Book a free consultation."

## 4. Service Page — Job Placement

**Goal:** convert a job seeker who wants an actual job placed for them.

1. **Hero** — Paper background, text on the left and image on the right, with a breadcrumb above. The line about candidates never paying belongs here, not buried in the FAQ — it's the first question every candidate has.
2. **What's Included** — Four to six tiles: resume and profile optimisation, interview coaching, employer matching, offer negotiation support, post-placement check-in, application tracking.
3. **What Changes** — A two-column contrast: what a job search looks like on your own, versus with us. Qualitative only — no salary or timeline promises.
4. **Process Steps** — Five numbered steps with realistic durations, **connected by "The Path"** as on Home: Consultation → Profile Build → Matching → Interviews → Offer.
5. **Success Story** — One real placement told as Situation / Approach / Result.
6. **FAQ Accordion** — Cost, timeline, what happens if the placement doesn't work out, industries covered.
7. **CTA Band** — "Start your job search today."

## 5. Service Page — Recruitment & Staffing

**Goal:** convert an employer looking to hire (temp, contract, or full-time staffing).

1. **Hero** — Mirrored layout from Job Placement (image left, text right) for variety across the five service pages while keeping the same component. Headline aimed at employers.
2. **What's Included** — Tiles: candidate sourcing, screening and vetting, staffing models (contract / temp / permanent), industry specialisations.
3. **Industries We Staff** — The sectors we recruit into, with typical roles for each. Employers scan for their own sector before reading anything else.
4. **How Engagements Work** — The commercial structure stated plainly: contingency for permanent, flat markup on contract, no retainer to start. Structure only, not rates — employers who can't find terms assume they're bad.
5. **Process Steps** — Numbered timeline **connected by "The Path"**: Requirement Analysis → Sourcing → Screening → Client Interviews → Onboarding.
6. **Why Employers Choose Us** — A stat-backed trust band (placement success, average time-to-fill, industries served) on the brand colour. If we don't genuinely track these, replace the band with an employer testimonial.
7. **FAQ Accordion** — Pricing model, minimum engagement, industries supported, turnaround.
8. **CTA Band** — "Tell us your hiring need", linking to Contact with the Employer option already selected.

## 6. Service Page — Talent Acquisition

**Goal:** distinguish from "Recruitment & Staffing" — this is strategic, higher-touch, usually for specialised or leadership roles.

1. **Hero** — On the surface tone rather than paper, a deliberate one-shade shift so this page reads as more premium than the two above without introducing any new colour. Headline emphasises precision over volume.
2. **How This Differs from Staffing** — A short comparison table placed high on the page, covering what each service does, timeline, candidate type, best-fit roles, and commercial terms. If a visitor can't tell the two apart in five seconds, both pages fail — this is the most important block here.
3. **What's Included** — Tiles: executive and specialised search, talent pipelining, market mapping, long-term partnership model. Market mapping gets a short block of its own, since describing that deliverable concretely is what justifies the premium framing.
4. **Process Steps** — The same timeline component **with "The Path"**, using search-specific step labels.
5. **FAQ Accordion** — Page-specific questions.
6. **CTA Band.**

## 7. Service Page — Background Verification

**Goal:** reassure employers (and candidates) that this is thorough, compliant, and fast. The most legally sensitive page on the site — write conservatively.

1. **Hero** — Paper background, text left and image right, with shield/checkmark iconography in the brand colour rather than the accent. Trust visuals should read as steady, not urgent.
2. **What We Verify** — Icon grid: employment history, education, criminal record check *(where legally permitted — this qualifier is not optional)*, reference checks, identity verification.
3. **Compliance Note** — A plain text block, no badge iconography. We state that every check begins with documented consent, how data is handled and retained, and that we don't display compliance badges we haven't earned. This last point is a deliberate contrast with the competitor, whose badge wall includes at least one certification that doesn't exist.
4. **Process Steps** — Numbered timeline **with "The Path"**: Request → Consent Collection → Verification → Report Delivery.
5. **FAQ Accordion** — Turnaround time, what's covered, data privacy handling, per-role customisation.
6. **CTA Band.**

## 8. Service Page — IT Training

**Goal:** a distinct audience — mainly students and early-career professionals wanting to upskill before placement. This page should feel more like a course catalogue than a service page.

1. **Hero** — Paper background, text left and a learning/classroom image right. Headline pitches the skill-to-job pipeline ("Train. Certify. Get Placed."). This is the one hero allowed a more energetic accent-coloured button, since this audience is earlier in the funnel and more exploratory.
2. **Programs Offered** — Card grid of tracks (Web Development, QA/Testing, Cloud/DevOps, Data Basics), each showing duration in the numeric face plus format and prerequisites — the two things a learner filters on first.
3. **Why Train With Us** — Ties back to Job Placement: graduates get priority access to our placement pipeline. This is the strongest differentiator against a generic training vendor. Set as a large pull-quote with the key phrase underlined in the accent colour. Always "priority access", never "guaranteed placement".
4. **What Certification Means** — What a graduate actually receives, and whether a track prepares for a vendor exam. Vague certification claims are the most common complaint about training providers.
5. **Pricing** — At minimum, that payment plans exist and how to get exact figures. A page with no price signal loses price-sensitive readers entirely.
6. **Curriculum Timeline** — Numbered timeline **with "The Path"**: Enroll → Learn → Practice → Certification → Placement Support. The clearest literal use of the site's path motif, since it ends at the same Job Placement pipeline.
7. **FAQ Accordion** — Duration, format, cost, prerequisites, and what placement support does and doesn't promise.
8. **CTA Band** — Accent-highlighted button: "Enrol Now" / "Book a Free Demo Class".

> If the tracks aren't real yet, this page ships as a single waitlist section instead — no durations, no prices, no curriculum until they exist.

## 9. Contact

**Goal:** low-friction conversion for both audiences, routed correctly.

1. **Page Header** — Surface background, title "Contact Us" and a short line: "Whether you're looking for a job or looking to hire, we're here to help."
2. **Contact Form + Info Split** — On the left, a form with an **"I am a…" choice** (Job Seeker / Employer / Training Applicant) that swaps in a couple of relevant fields per choice: employers get a company name, job seekers get a resume upload, training applicants pick a track. Keep the shared fields to name, email, and message — every extra field costs submissions. The response-time promise sits next to the submit button, and a one-line note on what we do with a submitted resume sits below the form. On the right, an info card with address, phone, email, and business hours. Stacks form-above-info on mobile.
3. **Map Embed** — Only if a physical office exists and can be visited; otherwise remove the block rather than showing a pin we don't occupy.
4. **Social / Alternate Contact** — Social icons plus the email and phone repeated for visitors who'd rather not use a form.
5. **FAQ Mini-Section** — Two or three quick "before you contact us" answers.

No final CTA band here — the form on this page *is* the call to action.

## Shared Components (all pages)

- **Header / Nav** — Logo on the left, links (Home, About, Services ▾, Contact) and a "Get Started" button on the right, which becomes a click-to-call link on mobile. Sticky on scroll, picking up a hairline bottom border rather than a heavy shadow.
  - **Services dropdown** — On desktop it opens on hover *and* on keyboard focus, never hover alone, as a panel listing all five service pages with an icon and label each. On tablet it becomes a single-column list. On mobile, "Services" is an expandable row inside the slide-out menu, revealing the five sub-links indented beneath it.
  - **Mobile menu** — A full-width slide-in panel that closes on outside click and on Escape, with the page behind it locked from scrolling.
  - No Careers, Insights, or Refer & Earn items until those pages exist and are maintained — a nav item leading to a thin page costs more than a missing one.
- **Footer** — Four columns on desktop, stacking to one on mobile: (1) logo, one-line pitch, social icons; (2) all five Services; (3) Company links, including Privacy Policy and Terms; (4) contact details. Registered entity name in the copyright line. No newsletter signup unless someone is genuinely going to send a newsletter.
- **CTA Band** — One reusable band (background, headline, button) with different wording per page. One action per band, never two competing buttons.
- **Stat Counter** — One reusable animated counter, fed from a single source so Home and About can never disagree. Numbers set in the numeric face.
- **FAQ Accordion** — One reusable accordion, with the questions for each page kept alongside the rest of the site content.

### Responsive behaviour — applies to every page and section above

Designed mobile-first, then adapted upward through tablet and desktop.

- **Hero** sections: text and image stacked on mobile, side by side on desktop.
- **Grids** (services, programs, team, differentiators): one column on mobile, two on tablet, three on desktop.
- **Zig-zag split sections** (Services detail, About story): always stack image-above-text on mobile, regardless of their left/right order on desktop.
- **Process timelines**: vertical stacked steps on mobile, a horizontal connected line on desktop.
- **Tables and comparisons**: collapse to stacked label-and-value pairs, or scroll sideways in their own container, rather than shrinking a wide table until it's unreadable.
- **Testimonial and logo rows**: swipeable on mobile, a fitted row on desktop.
- Every interactive component (dropdown, accordion, carousel) must work by touch, mouse, and keyboard. This is a hard requirement, not a nice-to-have, given the mobile-heavy job-seeker audience.
